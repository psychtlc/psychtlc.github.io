Why I Won't Be Renewing My PMP

  • Hiring
  • Project Management
Tara Glover May 24, 2026

I’m legitimately concerned by how many job postings now require a PMP. Let’s be brutally honest about what happens here. Companies use this certification as a lazy filter to reduce the massive flood of applications.

Are you really looking for the best project managers? Because right now, you’re filtering out some of the most knowledgeable, experienced, and pragmatic people in the field.

Good hiring managers will push back on that requirement when they can. I see it happen, and I respect it. It’s just still far too common across the board.

I won’t be renewing mine.

The PMP has become a purely pay-to-play credential. Its value rests entirely in demonstrating a willingness, and the financial ability, to keep paying PMI. True competence comes from navigating the weird, wonky bits of a project when everything goes sideways. A multiple-choice test cannot measure your ability to handle the messy stuff.

This practice is also completely antithetical to the stated goals of DEI. Look at what this requirement actually demands from candidates:

  • Disposable income to pay for exam prep, testing, and renewals.
  • Free time outside of working hours to study and maintain continuing education credits.
  • Generous employer support to subsidize the entire expensive process.

Those factors exclusively highlight privilege. Identifying top-tier talent requires a completely different approach.

We’ve all met people with the money and time to collect degrees or certifications who can barely find their way out of a paper bag. On the flip side, there are incredibly capable, brilliant people who simply don’t have the luxury of buying those credentials.

Teams need to drop the hiring shortcuts. Put the actual work in. Read the resumes. Talk to the candidates. Take the time to see through the noise and find the real people who can actually do the job.
