Stop Drowning in Invisible Work. Let AI Handle the Admin.

  • AI
  • Best Practices
Tara Glover October 24, 2026

How much of your week do you spend actually managing a project? Now, how much of your week do you spend just reporting on it?

Professional services teams spend an unsustainable amount of time doing paperwork instead of delivering value. For many delivery leads, Friday afternoon is dominated by a painfully familiar ritual. You chase late timesheets. You parse endless Slack histories. You manually update PowerPoint slides for stakeholder meetings. You’re drowning in invisible work.

This administrative burden distracts your most talented people from the work they’re actually paid to do.

My philosophy on automation is incredibly simple. Put AI on the admin. Put people on the judgment.

I recommend using AI inside the delivery function specifically to eliminate hours of low-value work from every single week. I recommend using it to draft weekly status updates, flag early resource burn, and turn meeting transcripts into concrete action items.

Look at the difference a simple shift makes:

  • Before: A delivery lead spends three hours manually synthesizing unstructured data from Jira and Asana into a coherent narrative.
  • After: A secure AI pipeline categorizes the live data. The delivery lead spends fifteen minutes verifying the draft, adjusting the tone, and sending it off.

Automating this pipeline is highly efficient. You just have to guard against the dangerous illusion of an automated green status.

AI tools are excellent at scraping meeting notes and formatting quantitative data. They’re absolutely terrible at navigating human dynamics. If your underlying project data is flawed, AI will happily generate a beautifully formatted report showing that everything is perfectly on track. It creates a massive false sense of security for your leadership team.

When a project starts to drift, you need a human. AI can’t negotiate scope truth. It can’t sit in a room with a client and have a deeply uncomfortable conversation about why the timeline is completely fucked. Those weird, wonky bits require a human operator. You need an experienced person to navigate the messy stuff and force an agreement on what is actually in scope right now.

If you’re ready to bring AI into your workflow, you need strict boundaries. Here is how I recommend building a safe pipeline:

  • Secure your data: Nothing commercially sensitive ever goes into a tool your business hasn’t explicitly approved. You work strictly inside your own stack and your own data rules.
  • Clean up your inputs: AI synthesis is only as good as the data it ingests. Establish clear hygiene standards for your PM tools. If your team ignores their tickets, your automated draft will be entirely useless.
  • Maintain absolute accountability: The delivery lead remains the sole owner of the status. The human reviewer checks every single line before it ever reaches a client.

Make a move to automate your reporting pipeline. Pull your delivery managers out of the spreadsheets. Free them up to focus on proactive risk mitigation, stakeholder alignment, and the high-value consulting that actually saves projects. Give them the time to do their real jobs, and see what happens to your delivery success.
