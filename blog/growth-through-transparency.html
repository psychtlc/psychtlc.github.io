Growth Through Transparency: A Blueprint for Leadership and Project Success

  • Leadership
Tara Glover August 24, 2023

Throughout my career, I’ve noticed one practice that consistently fixes broken teams across every level and industry. It works for an individual contributor fixing pesky bugs. It works for an executive leading a massive department. The secret sauce is always transparency.

The more access I have to information, the better I feel. The more comfortable I feel asking for context, the better work I do. It really is that simple.

Are you hoarding information without realizing it? Creating a culture of transparency starts with a hard look at your internal communication. Private channels create information silos. They also create trust silos. We end up trusting the people inside the private chat and distrusting the people outside of it.

Look at the difference a simple shift makes:

  • Before: Teams default to private messages. Everyone is siloed and guessing at the bigger picture.
  • After: Conversations happen in open channels. People get answers faster. Everyone has the context they need to navigate the messy stuff.

Make a move to open up your team’s chat platform. Let everyone have a say in what channels exist. Give them the autonomy to join the ones they care about. Being granted that access goes a long way in showing a team member that their voice actually matters.

If we can’t build trust and psychological safety, we can’t get the best work from our teams. Period. Full stop. This gets incredibly difficult when you join a new team. You have to admit when you’re struggling to learn something new. Admitting that difficulty normalizes the natural valleys of growth. It makes it safe for your team to ask for help when they hit a wall.

Think about the classic friction between Product and Engineering. These teams are often heavily siloed. They lack a culture of cross-functional collaboration. They stop asking questions. This quickly leads to massive misunderstandings. Product might promise a feature that Engineering never had on the roadmap. Engineering might understand technical constraints that Product knows nothing about. Both teams are operating without critical context, and it fucks up the entire workflow.

This is exactly where transparency becomes your best tool. You have to come together from a perspective of mutual benefit. The product needs to succeed for all of us to succeed. We’re working toward a shared goal that we can only achieve together. We’re weighing our options to find the path that works best for everyone.

It feels embarrassing sometimes to ask a ton of questions. It feels incredibly vulnerable to show people that you don’t have all the answers. Do it anyway. That vulnerability is exactly what cultivates a healthy, BS-free culture. Make a move to open up your communication, see what happens, and adjust from there.
